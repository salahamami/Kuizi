Your reference, turned into principles

You pointed to Claude Code and claude.com/fr/programs/startups as clean, with a good vision and calm workflows. I studied the page at 1440 px and 390 px. These are the principles behind it, written so that a learning product for 11–15-year-olds can use them without borrowing its look.

What we do not take

  • Anthropic's colours, typefaces, mark and illustrations. Kuizi keeps its star, its own palette and an Arabic-first type pairing.
  • The centred hero. Arabic-first screens are aligned to the start edge, and long centred lines are harder for young readers.
  • Photography and video of people: no photos of minors (MR-M2), and video costs prepaid data.
  • Headlines that fade in from light grey as you scroll: while they fade, the grey words are below 4.5:1, which fails WCAG for children with low vision.

How the three directions measure up. A is the calm, editorial direction built on these principles, adapted to children: it follows all six. B follows restraint and one idea, but adds a second hue, a shadowed card and more shapes. C is furthest away: one idea and a clear flow, but a large saturated surface breaks restraint and calm.

What all three share (not up for debate in this step)

On the screen, and why each element earns its place

  • One mission: subject, notion, one sentence of why, three steps, a duration. The only primary action starts it (principle 2).
  • "Une autre matière ce soir": autonomy without a catalogue. Band A gets two suggestions; band B can pick any subject.
  • The week as a goal of evenings that never resets to zero. No streak (MR-S5), no score, no percentage (MR-P3), no ranking (MR-S1).
  • "Besoin d'aide" / «أحتاج مساعدة» in the top bar of every pupil screen: the safeguarding button with helplines (MR-G2). Homework help lives inside exercises, under a different name ("Un indice").
  • Three tabs only: Aujourd'hui, Mes progrès, Moi. "What your parents see" lives in Moi (MR-F3).
  • The concours appears as "week 3 of 8" in band B's season view, never as a day counter (MR-S9).

Not on the screen, on purpose

  • No streak flames, badges, coins or mascot (principle 3, MR-T4).
  • No subject colours: subjects are named, not colour-coded, so the screen keeps one brand hue.
  • No notification bell: band A gets no pushes by default (MR-N1).
  • No predicted grade, no "you are behind", no comparison with classmates.

Craft rules applied to every phone

  • Spacing only from 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 px. Touch targets 48 px (help, links, tabs, buttons).
  • At most five type sizes and two weights per direction; Arabic in its own face and line height, never letter-spaced.
  • Neutrals carry the interface; one brand scale; B adds one marker hue for "you are here", always with a shape.
  • Maths, digits and the drawings of the physical world stay left to right inside Arabic. Arrows and the layout mirror.
  • Every state has a shape or words as well as a colour. Contrast is computed below for each direction.

Semantic colours (shared, not used on Today)

Success, attention and danger exist for meaning only (a saved answer, an account problem), always with an icon and words. "Not yet" in an exercise is never red.

A. Cahier: the evening page

Tonight's mission written as a sentence on a calm page, like the first line of a good school notebook. Typography does the work. One ink colour, no boxes. This is the calm, editorial direction built on the principles of your reference, adapted to children.

Why this direction

Tunisian pupils write in blue ink in their cahiers, and teachers correct in red. Kuizi takes the ink and leaves the red pen behind: a mistake gets an ink note, never a red mark. The mission is a sentence, because a coach speaks; it is not a widget.

A Naskh display face (Markazi Text) makes the Arabic look like it belongs, the way it looks in a good textbook, not translated. The interface text stays in IBM Plex Sans Arabic, kept from v1. The week's evenings are ink dots in the margin. Notebook ruling separates the three steps, so there are no boxes.

Serves most: 2 one next step, 3 calm, 5 Arabic first. Less: 4, because progress is quiet here.

An 11-year-old and a 15-year-old

Lina (11): calm, readable, grown-up in a way that flatters her. The drawing of two breads makes fractions concrete. Risk: without good illustration it looks like homework.

Adam (14): serious and respectful; the most "not for kids" of the three without being cold. The season line ("Révisions Neuvième · semaine 3 sur 8") replaces the countdown.

Risks

  • The least instant delight. It needs a good illustrator and fine typography, or it looks plain.
  • A serif display face may feel old-fashioned to some teenagers. To test.

Palette

The brand would move from v1 cyan to ink blue (encre 9, #1B4BA9). That is a brand decision (T231).

Ce soir, on compare des fractions.

Display: Markazi Text 600 (Arabic 44/64, Latin 40/44; the Arabic is set larger because its letters are smaller on the body). Interface: IBM Plex Sans Arabic and IBM Plex Sans 400 and 600, at 14, 16 and 20. Both are on Google Fonts under the SIL Open Font Licence.

Icons

Lucide, 24 px (16 and 20 in dense rows), 1.5 px stroke to match the ink line.

Illustration

Monoline ink drawings, 1.5 px, in one colour. Parts that carry meaning are inked solid (7.4:1 on paper), never a pale tint that would fall below 3:1. They show everyday Tunisian objects that carry the notion: a tabouna cut into parts for fractions, a market balance for equations, an olive branch for SVT. Children drawn as they are (skin tones, hijab or not, glasses, a wheelchair). Commissioned from a Tunisian illustrator. The drawings here are geometric placeholders.

Motion

When an evening is done, its dot fills like ink soaking into paper (200 ms, ease-out) and the tick draws itself (150 ms). Reduced motion: the dot is simply filled.

B. Ligne: the week as a line

The week drawn as a transit line. Evenings already done are stations passed; tonight is where you are, marked with the brand's nuqta; tomorrow is the next stop.

Why this direction

It makes progress visible in space: the most "felt" progress of the three. It uses the brand's own grammar: the nuqta (the rhombus at the heart of the star) becomes "you are here", and the line is the "way forward from any concept" of the brand idea. It keeps the v1 cyan (lagune 9, #026E89, next to v1's #0A6F8C).

The same component scales to the concours: in band B's season view the line holds the week's subjects and Saturday's practice paper, and the revision plan (C30) is the same line drawn in weeks.

Serves most: 4 effort you can see, 2. Less: 3, because there are more elements on screen.

An 11-year-old and a 15-year-old

Lina (11): the most fun and the clearest picture of "my week". She can see that Wednesday was a rest day and nothing broke.

Adam (14): risks feeling like a children's game. The band B version earns its place by showing the real week of revision, practice paper included.

Risks

  • Closest to well-known gamified "paths". It must stay a straight transit line with labelled stations, never a winding trail of bubbles.
  • More to build and to make accessible: the line must be a real list for screen readers, and it mirrors in Arabic.
  • The marker yellow is only 1.6:1 on the page, so it always carries a dark outline and the rhombus shape.

Palette

Keeps the v1 brand hue. Soleil is used only for the "you are here" marker, always with its outline.

C. Élan: one big thing

Tonight's mission is one big tile: tap anywhere on it to start. The first problem of the evening is the picture.

Why this direction

The most direct answer to "what do I do now?": the whole tile is the button, 358 × 448 px. The maths itself is the hero image, so no decorative illustration is needed, and the left-to-right formula inside right-to-left text is shown with pride rather than hidden.

Violet is the one hue that collides with none of the semantic colours, so the brand can never be mistaken for "success" or "danger".

Serves most: 2 one next step, very strongly. Less: 3, because a large saturated surface is not calm; and 4.

An 11-year-old and a 15-year-old

Adam (14): fast and confident, "an app for me", close to the sports and music apps he already uses.

Lina (11): clear to tap, but the big block of colour and the few words may feel cold, and a formula as the first thing she sees can feel like a test before she has started.

Risks

  • A large saturated surface at 21:00 in a dark room is tiring: the v1 "dark panel" problem in another colour.
  • Little room for the "why", which is what makes a coach a coach.
  • Without fine craft it slides into a generic fitness-app look.

Palette

The brand would move from cyan to violet (iris 9, #5A3BD5). That is a brand decision (T231).

Recommendation: A, Cahier, with two borrowings

  1. Calm is the scarcest thing for this audience. Eleven-year-olds facing a selective exam (about 11 % of Sixième candidates admitted in 2026) need the product to lower the temperature. A does that with paper, ink and one sentence.
  2. Arabic first you can see. A Naskh display face makes the Arabic version the original, not the translation, which is what families of pupils taught maths in Arabic will expect.
  3. It grows from 11 to 15 without a "kids theme". Band A gets more illustration and audio; band B gets denser text. Same structure.
  4. Cheapest to build, to make accessible and to run on a 2 GB Android with a prepaid data plan: mostly text, few graphics.
  5. A brand story parents can retell: "Kuizi writes in ink and never uses the red pen."
  6. It follows the reference you chose on all six principles (restraint, whitespace, editorial hierarchy, purposeful illustration, one idea, calm flow) while staying Kuizi: its own star, ink palette, Arabic-first type and a child's reading level.

Borrow from B: the line, for the revision plan screen (C30), where seeing weeks as stations is genuinely useful. Borrow from C: make the whole mission block one large tappable target, not just the button.

What would change my mind: if, in step 2, 6e pupils choose B over A clearly in a preference test ("which one would you open tonight?") and A scores below 4 of 5 on the face scale, A must gain warmth (illustration, the line as the week) before it is built. I propose testing A and B side by side with five pupils per band.
