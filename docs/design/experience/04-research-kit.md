**Why adults first.** Rounds 0 and 1 answer different questions. Parents and teachers can be heard now; children need the protocol. Round 0 alone already answers questions 1, 2 and 5 of section 6.

**A legal point for counsel.** MR-X1 asks for the tutor's consent **and** the article 28 condition for research with children. The protocol below is designed to collect **no personal data** at all: no names, no recording by default, notes coded "6e-P1", no photos. Counsel should confirm whether such anonymous sessions still need the family judge's authorisation. If they do, round 1 waits for it.

## 2. Protocol for sessions with children (summary for NEW-11)

Before every session, the facilitator ticks each line. **If one line is missing, the session does not happen.**

- [ ] The parent has read the information sheet and signed the consent form (section 5), declaring their capacity (MR-C2).
- [ ] The article 28 condition is met, or counsel has confirmed it is not needed for anonymous sessions.
- [ ] The child has said yes on the assent card, after it was read aloud. A "no" or "pas maintenant" ends the session kindly.
- [ ] Two adults are present, one of them trained in safeguarding. **Under 13: a parent is in the room** for the whole session.
- [ ] No audio unless the parent ticked the separate box. **Never video, never photos** of the child.
- [ ] Notes carry a code ("6e-P3"), never a name, school or address. They are typed up without identifiers and the paper is destroyed within a week; the typed notes are deleted three months after analysis.
- [ ] The facilitator knows the safeguarding route (T222, helpline 1809 to be confirmed) and stops the session if a child is distressed.
- [ ] Any prototype uses synthetic accounts and invented names only.

**During the session:** sit side by side, not facing the child like an examiner. Say "il n'y a pas de bonne réponse" more than once. Never test the child's school knowledge. Thank them for what they teach us.

**The five-face scale.** A printed card with five drawn faces, from "pas du tout" to "beaucoup", with the words written under each face in both languages. It is used to ask "comment tu te sens quand…" and "est-ce que ça te plaît". Drawn faces, not emoji characters.

## 3. Interview guides (30 minutes each)

Timings: 0–3 welcome and consent check · 3–8 warm-up · 8–20 core questions · 20–27 looking at the screens · 27–30 close. Follow-up prompts: « Tu peux m'en dire plus ? », « Montre-moi », « Et la dernière fois, c'était comment ? ». Ask about real past events, never "would you use…".

### 5.4 Adults (parents and teachers interviewed alone)

A short version of 5.1: purpose, 30 minutes, anonymous notes, audio only if ticked, stop at any time, deletion within three months, contact. Teachers are interviewed as individuals, outside their school's name, until the school-use question is settled (MR-E8, NEW-10).

## 6. The five questions whose answers would most change the design

| # | Question | If the answer is… | …the design changes like this |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Whose phone, and when?** Does a 6e pupil use a parent's shared phone in the evening, or their own? On wifi or prepaid data? | Mostly a shared parent phone | A quick switch between parent and pupil on one phone; sign-in by profile, not by password; reminders only to the parent; small offline downloads. If pupils have their own phone, band B's own reminders matter more |
| 2 | **Who follows the schoolwork, who decides, who pays, and is the legal tutor involved?** | The mother follows, the father consents and pays | The first run invites the second parent; the weekly summary goes to the follower, in their language; consent and follow-up become two roles |
| 3 | **When a child is stuck, what happens and who helps?** | They ask a sibling or give up; parents can't help with the maths | The one-minute lesson and hint ladder carry the weight, and "Un exemple semblable" comes first. If parents do help, the summary includes a "how to explain it" card |
| 4 | **Does the concours pressure come from home, school or peers, and does seeing "days left" help or hurt?** | 9e pupils want a day count; 6e pupils are frightened by it | A day count opt-in for band B only, never for band A (product owner question 4). If both bands find it stressful, the week plan stays the only view |
| 5 | **How is maths written and spoken in Arabic-medium classes (Latin letters, direction, French terms), and which language do pupils want the interface in?** | Teachers write Latin letters left to right; pupils want an Arabic interface | Confirms the bidirectional maths rules and Arabic as the default. If teachers write some formulas right to left, or pupils prefer French menus, item rendering and the language settings change |
