## How to read this

- Every figure cites a source in brackets, for example [1]. The numbered list with links is at the end.
- **Estimate** means Kuizi's own calculation; the formula is shown next to it. Estimates are not facts and must not be quoted publicly.
- **Confidence**: **High** = official figure (INS, Ministry of Education, Journal officiel) or a ministry figure that several outlets report identically. **Medium** = one press report of an official figure, or a credible organisation's survey. **Low** = a statement with no published method, promotional content, or our own extrapolation.
- Names used in Tunisia: the 6e concours is called *la Sixième* (مناظرة السيزيام, officially مناظرة الدخول إلى المدارس الإعدادية النموذجية). The 9e exam is called *la Neuvième* (مناظرة النوفيام), or the DFEEB, the diploma that ends basic education. Both exams are **optional**.
- Most sources are Tunisian press articles quoting the ministry: we found no recent official statistical yearbook with figures per grade. Figures can differ slightly from one outlet to another. Where they do, both are given.

### Recommendation in five points

1. **Position Kuizi as the coach that shows, concept by concept, what to practise next**, all year from 6e to 9e, with a concours mode for families who choose the Sixième or the Neuvième. Sell to parents. Never promise admission: only about 11% of the pupils who sat the Sixième in 2026 were admitted to a collège pilote.
2. **Start with the Sixième, in maths and *éveil scientifique* (early science)**. It is the larger exam (about 62,000 candidates a year against 32,000), it fits what the Kuizi brain already does, and we found no paid Tunisian product that adapts practice at this level. The content must be in Arabic, because maths and sciences are taught in Arabic up to 9e. Add the Neuvième next. Arabic, French and English are three of the five subjects in both exams; they need a content partner or a later phase.
3. **Price tests:** a free tier (diagnostic and a little daily practice), **25 TND a month** (also test 19 and 29), a **Concours Pass at 129 TND** for January to June (also test 99 and 149), and **199 TND a year**. For private schools, **30–50 TND per pupil per year**. Free solidarity places, given out through schools and associations so that Kuizi never collects income data. These prices sit below the official in-school lesson (35 TND per subject per month) and below two months of average private-lesson spending.
4. **Channels and payment:** private schools first (811 private primary schools and 601 private collèges and lycées), parent-facing Facebook and YouTube content (never ads aimed at under-18s), and parents' and consumer associations. Sell on the web with Tunisian payment rails (Flouci, D17/e-Dinar, domestic cards), school invoices and prepaid codes. Most Tunisian cards cannot pay app-store subscriptions in foreign currency.
5. **Timing:** with the current roadmap, the launch (Sprint 18, which starts on 2027-06-14) falls in the week of the 2027 exams, after that year's buying season. Proposal for the product owner: a free pilot, with parents' consent, in February–June 2027 with a few partner private schools, then a paid launch at the September 2027 rentrée, before the concours registration window (late October to November 2027).

## 1. Market size

### 1.1 How the school system is organised

- Basic education lasts nine years: six years of primary (1re–6e) and three years of collège (7e–9e). Lycée then lasts four years and ends with the Bac [56].
- **Maths and sciences are taught in Arabic up to 9e and in French at lycée** [56]. This comes from the Charfi reform of the early 1990s, as described in an opinion piece by the president of the parents' association. Mathematical symbols are written in Latin (French) notation throughout. Confidence: Medium; check against the official curriculum texts with the curriculum agent. For Kuizi, this means Arabic-first content for 6e–9e maths and science, with formulas set left to right inside right-to-left text.
- **Subjects in the 2026 exams** [1]:
  - Sixième: Arabic and English on day 1, French and *éveil scientifique* on day 2, maths on day 3; 4 h 45 in total.
  - Neuvième: Arabic and English, then French and SVT (life and earth sciences), then maths; 8 hours in total.
- The official threshold for a pilot school is an average of 15/20 [1] [6]. The ministry has sometimes admitted pupils below it (see 1.3).

What the tables show:

- **The Sixième is the bigger moment:** about 57,000–64,000 candidates a year, against about 33,000 for the Neuvième. In 2026, 94,454 pupils registered for the two exams together [1].
- **Only part of each grade registers.** About 29.5% of 6e pupils in 2024 [11] and 30.7% in 2026 (estimate: 62,450 ÷ 203,508). For 9e, 19.6% in 2025 [9] and 18% in 2026, which La Presse called a record abstention [5]. Most families at these grades are therefore not concours buyers. They are buyers of help through the year (61–67% pay for private lessons, see section 2).
- **Selectivity (2026, estimate):** 5,317 admitted ÷ 49,661 who sat = **10.7%** for the Sixième; 6,063 ÷ 25,301 = **24.0%** for the Neuvième.
- **The rules move.** In 2026 the ministry admitted 38% more pupils than the announced places for collèges pilotes, and 62% more for lycées pilotes, without a published explanation. In July 2025 it admitted every Sixième pupil with 14/20 or more [7]. In 2024 a threshold of 14/20 was reported [13], while the official rule is 15/20 [6]. In 2020 the minister of the time said the Sixième might become compulsory [57]; it is still optional. A Conseil supérieur de l'éducation et de l'enseignement was set up in August 2026 to advise on the direction of education [60].
- There are 54 pilot collèges and lycées [1].

### 1.4 Public and private schools

- **INS, school year 2021-22** [18]:
  - Primary: 1,321,163 pupils, of whom 111,710 were in private schools (**8.5%**).
  - Collège and lycée: 1,090,896 pupils, of whom 94,373 were in private schools (**8.7%**), plus 9,307 in trade schools.
  - In 2020-21 the private shares were 7.8% and 7.7% [17].
- **We did not find a split for collège alone.** Much of private secondary education is at lycée level; that is an assumption to check.
- **More recent primary figures:**
  - About 128,000 pupils in private primary schools in 2024, six times the 2009 figure, according to an Observatoire tunisien de l'économie study reported by La Presse [19].
  - The same article cites a 2025 field survey estimating that about 8% of primary pupils attend a private school [19].
  - Business News gives 134,191 pupils in 2024, without a source [20].
  - The president of the parents' association put the private share at about 10% of pupils in 2026 [31].
- **Schools:**
  - 811 authorised private primary schools in September 2025, up from 774 in 2024 [22].
  - 601 authorised private collèges and lycées on 13 September 2025, up from 584 in December 2024 [21].
  - Both are concentrated around Tunis and in Sousse: Tunis 1 and 2 had 125 private primary schools, Ben Arous 82 and Ariana 66; Tunis 1 had 56 private collèges and lycées, Sousse 50 and Ben Arous 45 [21] [22].
- **Fees:** two examples for 2025-26 put private primary tuition at 9,095 TND (Sfax) and 11,000 TND (Sousse) a year, or 13,000–15,700 TND with canteen and other services [20]. Confidence: Low (two examples, not a survey).
- **What it means:** private schools teach about one pupil in ten or twelve. They are concentrated in the urban, connected, paying segment that Kuizi should reach first, and a school can sign one contract for a whole 6e class.

### 1.5 Demographic outlook

- **Births have fallen every year since 2015** [23]:
  - down about 3,000 from 2015 to 2016;
  - down about 7,800 a year from 2016 to 2019;
  - down 21,707 from 2019 to 2020;
  - down about 13,000 a year from 2020 to 2024.
- The INS describes 2010–2014 as a "mini baby boom" [23]. There were 147,242 births in 2023 and 133,322 in 2024 [24].
- The INS gives a fertility rate of 1.7 children per woman from 2022 civil records [23], and a later INS projection gives 1.54 for 2024 [62]. The INS expects a slight recovery in births from 2027 [62].
- **What it means for 6e (estimate):**
  - Today's 6e pupils were born around 2014, at the peak, so this is the largest cohort Kuizi will see.
  - The cohort reaching 6e should shrink slowly from about 2027-28 (children born in 2016) and quickly from about 2031-32 (children born in 2020).
  - Children born in 2024 reach 6e around 2035-36: 133,322 births against 203,508 pupils in 6e today is about **one third fewer**. This ignores repeaters and migration.
  - Growth must come from market share and from more grades, not from more pupils.

The year-round market is several times larger than the concours market.

## 2. Willingness to pay

### 2.1 How many families pay, and how much

- **FTDES study, September 2023** [25]. Share of pupils taking private lessons: 67% in primary, 61% in collège, 80% in lycée. Average monthly cost: 94 TND per primary pupil, 74 TND per collège pupil, 120 TND per lycée pupil. National total: 1.468 bn TND over ten months. The press coverage gives no sample size or method; the report itself should be read before any public use. Confidence: Medium. This is the only published, quantified study we found.
- **INS household survey 2021** [34]. Households reported spending 81 TND per person per year on education, 1.5% of their budget (84 TND and 2.2% in 2015). The survey ran from March 2021 to March 2022, during COVID. This is far below the FTDES figures. Our reading, not established: informal cash payments for private lessons are under-reported in household surveys.
- **Statements in 2026** (Low confidence; no method published):
  - The president of the Association tunisienne des parents et des élèves estimates 300–600 TND per pupil per month, up to 2,000 TND in extreme cases, and 1.5–3 bn TND for the market. These figures are mostly about exam classes [30] [31].
  - For Bac pupils, La Presse reported up to about 1,000 TND per pupil, and 1,000–2,000 TND during the revision period [32]. This shows how far spending rises in exam years; 6e–9e spending is much lower.
  - The Organisation tunisienne pour l'orientation du consommateur (OTOC) reports prices up 15–20% in a year: 80–90 TND per subject per month during the school year, and 150–200 TND per subject during revision periods [29].
- **Who buys:** demand rises with household wealth and with family size (Rahmouni and Saidi, using the INS household survey) [58]. In five MENA countries including Tunisia, it also rises with the pupil's age, the parents' education and urban residence (Fakih, Haimoun and Sleiman) [59].
- Tutoring is described as "a compulsory step" at every level, in public and private schools alike. Parents blame the quality of school; teachers blame disengaged parents [27].

### 2.3 Household budget

- **SMIG from 1 January 2026:** 470.251 TND (40-hour regime) and 554.736 TND (48-hour regime) a month. It rises to 493.304 and 582.400 TND in 2027 [35].
- **INS 2021** [34]:
  - Average consumer spending: 5,468 TND per person per year; 11,767 TND in the richest fifth and 2,014 TND in the poorest fifth.
  - Poverty rate: 16.6% nationally, 4.7% in Greater Tunis and 37.0% in the Centre-Ouest.
- **Inflation:** 5.6% year on year in September 2026 [36].
- **Reading the proposed prices:**
  - 25 TND a month is 4.5–5.3% of a SMIG, 71% of one subject's in-school lesson (35 TND), and about the price of one private group session.
  - 129 TND for the Concours Pass is less than two months of the average collège private-lesson spend (2 × 74 = 148 TND), and less than one subject during the revision peak (150–200 TND).

## 4. Channels and calendar

### 4.1 Devices and connectivity

- **INS, 2024 census** [48]:
  - Households: 95.5% have a mobile phone (2.31 phones per household on average), 40.4% have an internet connection, 26.1% have a computer, and 59.5% have a smart TV.
  - Home internet passes 50% only in Tunis (59.7%), Ariana (57.4%), Ben Arous (56.1%), Monastir (52.8%) and Sousse (50.3%). It is lowest in Sidi Bouzid (12.4%), Kasserine (14.8%), Jendouba (17.1%) and Kairouan (20.7%).
  - Literate people aged 10 and over who used the internet in the last three months: 76.5% (81.4% in towns, 61% in rural areas).
- **Children aged 10–17:** about 76% use the internet, on average 4 hours a day during the school year and 6 during holidays, and nearly 90% have access to a smartphone. These figures come from the national report on the situation of children (2024 edition, presented in February 2026), as quoted by the press [50] [51]. Confidence: Medium.
- **DataReportal, late 2025** [49]:
  - 10.4 million internet users (84.3% of the population).
  - Advertising audiences: Facebook 7.80 million, YouTube 7.83 million, Instagram 4.05 million, TikTok (18 and over) 6.0 million.
- **What it means:** design for the phone first (Android, little data, offline, works on a parent's shared phone). A computer version comes second. Usage outside the coastal cities depends on mobile data.

### 4.2 How parents choose: what we know and what we assume

**Evidence:**

- Tutoring is near universal and driven by doubts about school quality [27]. Better-off, urban and more educated families buy more [58] [59].
- Teachers are the main providers of tutoring. The rules forbid public-school teachers to give private lessons outside their schools, but this is widely ignored [27] [33]. A member of parliament is drafting a bill to ban private lessons altogether (announced in May 2026; not yet filed) [30].
- Official information reaches families through ministry platforms (six.education.tn and neuf.education.tn for registration), results by SMS, the press, and radio interviews (Jawhara FM, Diwan FM, Mosaïque FM) [4] [5] [30] [54].
- Facebook is the largest social network for adults, and so for parents [49].

**Assumptions not supported by the sources we found** (to test in interviews):

- Parents choose on the advice of the 6e teacher, other parents and class WhatsApp or Facebook groups.
- The decision to sit the Sixième is taken with the teacher during the registration window (October–November).
- Parents of collège pupils compare private-lesson cost per subject rather than per month.

**Draft channel plan** (no contact or spending without the product owner's approval):

1. **Private schools:** a school contract reaches a whole 6e or 9e class, and the school vouches for Kuizi. Start in Greater Tunis, Sousse, Monastir and Sfax, where connectivity and private schools are concentrated.
2. **Content for parents** on Facebook and YouTube: worked examples, "how the Sixième works", honest numbers on the exams. No ads targeted at under-18s; see the minors' data rules in risk 8.
3. **Parents' and consumer associations** (ATPE, ODC, OTOC) for credibility. They are vocal about the cost of private lessons.
4. **Teachers, through their schools,** using the class dashboard. No paid referrals to teachers for their own pupils (risk 9).
5. **Earned press and radio** at concours moments (registration, revision, results), not paid placements.

### 4.3 Payment

- "Tunisian credit cards are not approved for transactions in currencies other than the dinar." The exception is a state "digital technology charge card" for graduates. Cash on delivery remains the most common payment for online purchases [52]. **Most families therefore cannot easily pay an App Store or Google Play subscription.**
- **Local options:**
  - Flouci: nearly 500,000 downloads and more than 140,000 accounts in June 2026 [53].
  - La Poste's e-Dinar and its D17 app [52].
  - Domestic bank cards, which work for domestic online payments [52].

## 5. What this means for Kuizi

### 5.1 Positioning

**Draft statement (English, internal):** for parents of pupils in 6e to 9e, Kuizi shows, concept by concept, what the child already masters and what to practise next. It works all year on a phone and has a concours mode for the Sixième and the Neuvième. It does not replace the teacher or the tutor; it makes their time count.

**Draft lines for discussion.** Not for publication; the Arabic needs review by a native editor.

- FR: « Kuizi montre, notion par notion, ce que votre enfant maîtrise déjà et ce qu'il doit travailler ensuite, toute l'année, et pour le concours s'il décide de le passer. »

<div dir="rtl" lang="ar">

- AR: «كويزي يبيّن لكم، مفهومًا بمفهوم، ما يتقنه طفلكم وما عليه أن يتدرّب عليه بعد ذلك، طوال السنة، وللمناظرة إن قرّر اجتيازها.»

</div>

**Principles:**

- Diagnose, then practise, then tell the parent in plain words.
- Maths and science in Arabic, with Latin notation.
- Honest about the odds of admission.
- No promise of admission, no countdowns, no fear, no ranking against other children.

**Against the alternatives:**

| Alternative | Why a parent would still choose Kuizi |
| --- | --- |
| Private tutor | Costs less than one session a month, works every day between sessions, and shows the tutor and the parent exactly which concepts are weak. Kuizi works alongside the tutor rather than replacing them. |
| Taki Academy | Practice and diagnosis rather than more videos and live classes; a clear report for parents. |
| Free ministry platforms | Adaptive practice all year rather than content released just before the exam; a progress report for parents. |
| Past-paper books | Feedback on every answer, and a route back to the weak prerequisite concept. |
